# Content Calendar Planning

A content calendar is a short, dated list of what to post, where, and why. Its job is to make posting steady and purposeful, so every post helps the business rather than filling a slot.

## When to use

- Creating the first calendar for a business.
- The weekly content routine: planning the coming week.
- The person changes goals, launches something, or has a seasonal event coming.

## Step 1: Pick 3 to 5 themes

Themes are the recurring subjects the business is known for. Derive them from the Company Brief (what it sells, for whom, what makes it different). A good default mix:

| Theme | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Help | Teach the audience something useful | "3 ways to make your plants survive winter" |
| Behind the scenes | Build trust in the people and the craft | "How we pick our coffee beans" |
| Proof | Show results and happy customers (only real, approved ones) | "A customer story, shared with permission" |
| Offer | Tell people what they can buy and why now | "Our spring workshop has 5 places left" |
| Community | Join in local or industry moments | "Our stall at Saturday's market" |

Keep offers to at most one post in four. People follow accounts that help them, not ones that only sell.

## Step 2: Set the rhythm

Match the posting frequency the person chose. Spread posts across the week (for two a week, Tuesday and Thursday work well). Use one platform well before adding a second.

## Step 3: Fill the calendar

For a four-week calendar, write one row per post:

```md
| Date | Platform | Theme | Idea | Purpose | Needs from you |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tue 4 Mar | LinkedIn | Help | 3 mistakes first-time buyers make | Attract new customers | — |
| Thu 6 Mar | LinkedIn | Offer | Spring workshop dates | Fill workshop places | Confirm dates and price |
```

- "Purpose" says what the post should achieve: attract, build trust, sell, or keep customers.
- "Needs from you" lists any fact, photo or approval you need. Leave it as "—" when you need nothing.
- Add known dates: holidays, seasons, launches and events from the Company Brief or the person.

## Step 4: Check the calendar

- Every theme appears at least once every two weeks.
- No two offer posts in a row.
- Nothing depends on a fact you have not confirmed.
- The total matches the chosen frequency.

## Weekly upkeep

Each week: move the next week's rows into a plan, add anything new (news, launches, questions customers asked), drop ideas that are no longer timely, and add one new week at the end so the calendar always looks four weeks ahead. Note what worked last week if you can see results.

## Output

Save the calendar as a task document (`content-calendar` for the full calendar, `content-plan` for one week). Start with a two-line summary: the themes and the rhythm.
