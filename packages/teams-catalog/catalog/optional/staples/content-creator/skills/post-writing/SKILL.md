# Post Writing

Write posts that a real customer would stop to read, in the business's own voice. A post is good when its first line earns attention, it says one useful thing, and the reader knows what to do next.

## Before you write

1. Read the Company Brief for the business's words, offers and constraints.
2. Read the calendar row: platform, theme, idea and purpose.
3. Decide the one thing the reader should take away. If you cannot say it in one sentence, the idea is too broad: split it.

## Shape by platform

### LinkedIn post
- 80 to 200 words.
- First line: a specific claim, number or question that makes the reader want the second line. No greeting.
- Short paragraphs of one or two sentences, with a blank line between them.
- End with one clear next step or question.
- At most three relevant hashtags, at the end.

### X post
- One post of up to 280 characters. Keep it to one idea.
- A thread only when the idea truly needs steps; at most five posts, each able to stand alone.
- At most one or two hashtags, or none.

### Blog article (website)
- 500 to 900 words, with a title that says what the reader gets.
- An opening paragraph that names the reader's problem.
- Three to five sections with plain headings.
- A short ending with one next step (book, visit, reply, read more).
- Suggest a one-sentence meta description and a short URL slug.

## Tone

- **Friendly:** contractions, "you" and "we", warm but not gushing. One exclamation mark at most.
- **Professional:** clear and calm, no slang, no exclamation marks, confident without hype.
- **Playful:** light humour and wordplay, but never at a customer's expense, and never on serious subjects.

Whatever the tone: plain words, short sentences, no buzzwords ("synergy", "game-changer", "revolutionise"), and no filler openings ("In today's world...").

## Draft format

For each post, write:

```md
### <Date> — <Platform> — <Theme>
<The post, exactly as it will appear>

Image idea: <what to show, or "none">
Next step for the reader: <the action>
Facts to confirm: <list, or "none">
```

## Check before approval

- Every fact about the business is in the Company Brief, was in an approved post, or was confirmed by the person. Otherwise list it under "Facts to confirm" and do not publish yet.
- No invented reviews, quotes, statistics or results.
- Spelling of names, products and prices is exactly as in the Company Brief.
- Links work and go to the right page.
- It would not embarrass the business if a customer, a competitor or a journalist read it.
- It fits the platform's length.

## After publishing

Record the date, platform and link in the week's `content-plan` document. When results are visible, note which posts did well, so the next plan can use what worked.
