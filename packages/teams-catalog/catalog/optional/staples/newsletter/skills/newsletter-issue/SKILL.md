# Newsletter Issue

One issue, written so a subscriber can read it in three minutes and come away with something useful. The bar: the subject line earns the open, the first item earns the scroll, and the reader would forward at least one item to a friend.

## When to use

- The weekly "Research and draft the issue" routine, when an issue is due.
- The owner asks for a special issue (a launch, an event, a seasonal offer).

## Is an issue due?

Before anything else, check the cadence and the `issue-log` document:

Judge by the next send day (the one this issue is for):

- **Weekly:** an issue is due every week.
- **Every two weeks:** due if the last issue went out 10 or more days before the next send day.
- **Monthly:** due if the next send day is the first send day of its month and no issue has gone out that month.

If no issue is due, close the task with one line ("No issue due this week; next one on <date>.") and stop.

## Procedure

1. **Read** the Company Brief, the newsletter plan (`plan` document) and the `issue-log`, so you know the format and what was covered recently.
2. **Gather** ideas from the last period: news in the topic area, questions customers asked, new products or offers the owner mentioned, seasonal moments. Note sources.
3. **Choose** two to four items. At least one must be useful without buying anything (a tip, a how-to, an answer to a common question). At most one offer.
4. **Write** the issue in the format below.
5. **Write three subject lines** and pick one as recommended.
6. **Check** with the list below.
7. **Save** as the task document `issue`, comment with the recommended subject line and anything to confirm, and set the task to `in_review` if anything needs confirming, otherwise mark it done.

## Format

```md
Subject: <recommended subject line>
Other options: <subject 2> / <subject 3>
Preview text: <40-90 characters that continue the subject line>

---

Hi <first name or "there">,

<Intro: two or three sentences. What this issue gives the reader.>

## <Item 1 heading>
<60-150 words. One idea. A link if useful.>

## <Item 2 heading>
...

## <Offer, if any>
<What, for whom, until when, how to get it. Exact facts only.>

<Sign-off: one line, then the sender's name.>

---
Facts to confirm: <list, or "none">
Sources: <list>
```

## Subject lines

- 30 to 50 characters. Specific beats clever: "5 ways to keep cut flowers fresh" beats "Our latest news".
- No all-caps, no "!!!", no "Free" in the first word (spam filters).
- Do not promise what the issue does not deliver.

## Check before approval

- Every fact about the business comes from the Company Brief, an earlier issue, or the owner. Otherwise list it under "Facts to confirm".
- Every link works and goes to the right page.
- Names, prices and dates are exactly right.
- The issue is readable in about three minutes (300 to 600 words).
- It would not embarrass the business if forwarded to anyone.

## After sending

Add a line to the `issue-log`: date, subject line, items covered, and later the open and click rates if the service shows them.
