# Research Sources

Use this skill whenever you state a fact in a brief, report or answer. The person will make business decisions on what you write, so every claim must be checkable.

## Source ranking

Prefer sources higher on this list. Use lower ones only to find leads, then confirm them higher up.

1. **Primary:** the company's own site, pricing page, press release, filing, product changelog; the official text of a law or notice; a government or regulator's statistics.
2. **Established reporting:** recognised trade publications and news outlets that name their sources.
3. **First-hand voices:** customer reviews, forum posts, job listings, social posts from the company itself. Good for signals and sentiment, weak for facts.
4. **Aggregators and opinion:** listicles, blogs that repeat other sources, AI-generated summaries. Use only to find a primary source.

## Rules

- **Date everything.** Write the publication date next to every source. If a page has no date, say "undated, checked <today>".
- **Two sources for anything surprising.** A big claim (a price change, an acquisition, a new law) needs two independent sources, or must be marked "one source only".
- **Quote numbers exactly** as the source gives them, with units and time period. Do not round in a way that changes the meaning.
- **Separate fact from reading.** Write "The competitor's pricing page now lists £29/month (was £39)" as fact, and "This suggests they are chasing smaller customers" as your interpretation.
- **Mark uncertainty plainly:** "unconfirmed", "rumour", "one source only", "estimate".
- **Never invent.** Never make up a fact, figure, quote, person, company or link. If you cannot find something, say "I could not find a reliable source for this."
- **Link to the specific page,** not the home page.

## Paywalls, logins and private data

- Do not try to get around a paywall or login. Use the free summary or find another source, and say what you could not access.
- Do not collect personal data about private individuals. Research companies, markets and public figures acting in their public roles.

## Conflicting sources

When sources disagree, report both, say which you trust more and why (newer, primary, more specific), and do not pick a side silently.

## Citation format

In the "Sources" section of a document:

```md
- <Title> — <Publisher> — <date> — <link>
```

Inline, link the key words of the claim to its source, and keep the date in the sentence when it matters ("On 3 March, <Competitor> announced...").
